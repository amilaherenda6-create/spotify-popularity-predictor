The Problem

Every year, millions of songs are uploaded to Spotify. Most are never heard. A handful become global hits.

The question every artist, producer, and label manager asks:

  • 🎯 Will this song be a hit? (Yes or No)
  • 📊 How popular will it be? (on a scale of 0 to 100)


Our approach: Analyse only the audio characteristics of a song — no artist name, no label, no marketing budget.

If the sound itself contains the secret to success, our tool will find it.

Source: Spotify Tracks Dataset (Kaggle 2022)

Our Approach

We answer two questions simultaneously:

🎯 Yes/No Prediction

Will this song be popular?

A score of 70 or above = Popular ✅

Below 70 = Not yet popular

📊 Score Prediction

How popular will it actually be?

Predicts the exact number (0 to 100)

The closer to 100, the bigger the hit

What We Found in the Data

Finding 1 — Most songs are NOT popular

Only 1 in 20 songs reaches the top tier (score ≥ 70)

  • 5,465 popular songs out of 106,907 total
  • This makes prediction genuinely hard — hits are rare

Finding 2 — Most songs score low

The majority sit between 20 and 40 out of 100. Many new or obscure tracks score 0.

Finding 3 — No single signal decides everything

Signal Connection
Loudness Moderate positive
Energy Slight positive
Acousticness Slight negative
Instrumentalness Moderate negative

Finding 4 — Genre matters most

Pop, dance, and hip-hop score higher than classical, ambient, or sleep music.

Predicting popularity requires combining many small signals — no single factor decides it.

What Matters Most?

The audio signals that most influence popularity:

Boosts popularity ↑

  • 🎵 Genre — pop, dance, hip-hop lead
  • 🔊 Loudness — louder tracks score higher
  • 💃 Danceability — easy to dance to = more streams
  • ⚡ Energy — intense, driving tracks perform better

Reduces popularity ↓

  • 🎹 Instrumentalness — songs without vocals score lower
  • 🎸 Acousticness — electronic/produced sounds win
  • 😴 Duration — very long tracks score lower
  • 🎙 Speechiness — too much talking hurts


We also created new signals by combining existing ones — for example, a “club music score” (danceability × energy) — which improved predictions beyond what any single measurement could achieve.

Honest Limitations

What the model does well ✅

  • Identifies popular vs unpopular songs reliably
  • Finds 76 out of every 100 genuine hits
  • Useful as a first filter when reviewing large numbers of songs
  • Confirms that genre, energy, and danceability are real, measurable signals

What the model cannot do ❌

  • Predict viral hits — timing, social media moments, and cultural trends are invisible to audio data
  • Evaluate artistic quality — it rewards commercial conventions, not creativity or originality
  • Work for niche genres — rare genres have too few examples to learn from
  • Replace human judgment — past patterns cannot predict future trends

The honest number

Audio features explain only 38% of why a song becomes popular. The remaining 62% is artist fame, marketing, timing, and everything outside the sound.

Recommendations

For artists & producers

  • Choose your genre intentionally — commercial genres attract more streams
  • Aim for high energy and danceability if mainstream success is the goal
  • Keep tracks under 4 minutes
  • Produce loudly (within streaming platform standards)

These are patterns from data — not rules. Originality still matters.

For labels & A&R teams

  • Use this tool as a first-pass filter on demo submissions
  • Apply it to surface newly uploaded tracks before any listening data exists
  • Do not use it as the final gatekeeper — human taste remains essential


Key takeaway: Popularity is about 38% sound and 62% everything else.
