The Problem

Every year, millions of songs are uploaded to Spotify. Most are never heard. A handful become global hits.

The question every artist, producer, and label manager asks:

  • 🎯 Will this song be a hit? (Yes or No)
  • 📊 How popular will it be? (on a scale of 0 to 100)

Our approach: Analyse only the audio characteristics of a song — no artist name, no label, no marketing budget.

If the sound itself contains the secret to success, our tool will find it.

The Data

Source: Spotify Tracks Dataset (Kaggle 2022) · 114,000 tracks · 21 audio features · 114 genres

What we worked with

Our Approach

We answer two questions simultaneously:

🎯 Yes/No Prediction

Will this song be popular?

A score of 70 or above = Popular ✅

Below 70 = Not yet popular

📊 Score Prediction

How popular will it actually be?

Predicts the exact number (0 to 100)

The closer to 100, the bigger the hit

We tested 4 approaches — from simple to powerful:

Step What we tried
1 Random guessing — our starting point
2 Logistic Regression / Ridge Regression
3 Decision Tree (depth = 6)
4 XGBoost — fine-tuned for best results

What We Found

Finding 1 — Most songs are NOT popular

Only 1 in 20 songs reaches the top tier (score ≥ 70)

  • 5,465 popular songs out of 106,907 total
  • Hits are genuinely rare — prediction is hard

Finding 2 — Most songs score low

Majority cluster between 20 and 40. Many new or obscure tracks score 0.

Finding 3 — No single signal decides everything

Signal Connection
Loudness Moderate positive
Energy Slight positive
Acousticness Slight negative
Instrumentalness Moderate negative

Finding 4 — Genre is the strongest predictor

Pop, dance, and hip-hop score highest.

What Matters Most?

Audio signals that most influence popularity:

Boosts popularity ↑

  • 🎵 Genre — pop, dance, hip-hop lead
  • 🔊 Loudness — louder = more streams
  • 💃 Danceability — playlists reward it
  • ⚡ Energy — intense tracks perform better

Reduces popularity ↓

  • 🎹 Instrumentalness — no vocals = lower score
  • 🎸 Acousticness — electronic beats win
  • 😴 Duration — very long tracks score lower
  • 🎙️ Speechiness — too much talking hurts

We also created new signals by combining features — a “club score” (danceability × energy) improved predictions beyond any single measurement.

Honest Limitations

What the model does well ✅

  • Identifies popular vs unpopular songs reliably
  • Finds 76 out of every 100 genuine hits
  • Useful as a first filter for large song catalogues
  • Confirms genre, energy, and danceability are real signals

What the model cannot do ❌

  • Predict viral hits — timing and social media are invisible to audio data
  • Evaluate artistic quality — it rewards commercial patterns
  • Work for niche genres — too few training examples
  • Replace human judgment — past patterns cannot predict future trends

Audio features explain only 38% of why a song becomes popular.
The remaining 62% is artist fame, marketing, timing, and everything outside the sound.

Recommendations

For artists & producers

  • Choose genre intentionally — commercial genres attract more streams
  • Aim for high energy and danceability for mainstream success
  • Keep tracks under 4 minutes
  • Produce loudly (within streaming standards)

These are data patterns — not rules. Originality still matters.

For labels & A&R teams

  • Use this tool as a first-pass filter on demo submissions
  • Apply it to surface newly uploaded tracks before any listening data exists
  • Do not use it as the final gatekeeper — human taste remains essential

Key takeaway: Popularity is about 38% sound and 62% everything else.
